ONE ANOTHER CHRISTIANITY
Following Jesus means learning to live for one another.
Main Idea:
Because of God’s grace and mercy in Christ, we are transformed from a selfish, self-centered life into an other-centered life where we recognize that we belong to one another.
First let us see how this came to be.
I. THE GOSPEL TRANSFORMS US
Romans 12:1–3
Everything begins with what God has done for us.
Rom 12:1
Therefore, I urge you, brothers and sisters, in view of God's mercy, to offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God this is true worship.
Rom 12:2
Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind.
Because of God’s grace and mercy in Christ, we are called to true worship—to offer our lives to God as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to Him.
But God’s grace does not only change our relationship with Him. It changes the way we live.
Romans 12:2 says:
“Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind.”
God calls us to a life of transformation, not conformation.
The origin of the word “conform” traces back to the Latin word conformare, meaning "to shape, fashion, or fashion after."
To conform to the world therefore means allowing the world around us to shape us—to think, act, and live like everyone else. Conformation to the world therefore means- hinuhulma tayo ayon sa shape ng mundo.
The word “transform” traces back to the Latin word transformare, meaning "to change the shape of." Pag sinabing transform, binabago mo yung hugis o wangis.
Now yung transformation na binabanggit ni Paul sa Romans 12 hindi lang- hugis ang nais baguhin. The transformation that Paul referred to here is a transformation from the inside out.
This transformation begins in the mind.
Rom 12:2 Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind.
“Ang pagbabago ay nagsisimula sa pagbabago ng ating kaisipan. What we believe shapes how we think, and how we think influences how we live. That is why God does not only want to change what we do—He wants to renew the way we think.”
So from the inside out, God changes us through Jesus Christ.
Because of what Christ has done in us, our lives should become different.
We should not simply blend in with the world. The way we think, love, serve, forgive, and treat others should show that Jesus is changing us.
Jesus did not save us so we could simply fit in. He is transforming us so that we can stand out and reflect Him.
NOW one of the marks of this transformation is humility.
Romans 12:3 — “Do not think of yourself more highly than you ought, but rather think of yourself with sober judgment.”
Now notice again- how we are transformed into becoming humble people….  Humility involves what we think about ourselves. To be transformed into becoming humble, we are to think of ourselves NOT more highly than we ought. Paul is warning against pride and an exaggerated view of ourselves. Instead we are to think of ourselves in sober judgment.. thinking sensibly, soundly, or with proper judgment.
In simple terms:
Don’t think you are more important, more valuable, or better than others. See yourself honestly and humbly before God.
And so instead of having an inflated, independent view of ourselves we are to have a sense of need of each other.
Ibig sabihin, yung focus sa sarili mababago din! Magkakaroon ng transformation. This transformation…
II. TRANSFORMATION CHANGES “ME” INTO “WE”
This transformation to humility results in recognizing that we are members and parts of one body. Hindi isolated kundi connected.
Romans 12:5 — “So in Christ we, though many, form one body, and each member belongs to all the others.”
There is a transformation from having a high view of oneself, likely being self-absorbed and uninvolved, to recognizing our need for belonging to others.
It is a transformation: From a selfish, self-centered view to an other-centered view.
This is what we call: ONE ANOTHER CHRISTIANITY
We recognize that we belong to one another.
But how does this “One another Christianity” look like?
Paul then shows us what this One Another Christianity looks like in our relationships.
III: ONE ANOTHER CHRISTIANITY IS A LIFE OF LOVE
In Romans 12:9–16 Paul starts talking about LOVE. God’s people are to be transformed into becoming a people of love.
What loving one another looks like.
Now at this point I want you all to pay attention. Because we are going to be very practical. And I want for you to specifically think of how you can apply today's message
Loving one another is…
A: SERVING: It is serving  the Lord and one another.
Romans 12:11 — “Never be lacking in zeal, but keep your spiritual fervor, serving the Lord.”
Sabi ng 5 love languages. Service is 1 of the 5 love languages. Serving is an expression of love.
Words of Affirmation: Using spoken or written words—like compliments, appreciation, or encouragement—to validate and uplift someone.
Receiving Gifts: Valuing the thought, effort, and symbolic meaning behind a tangible item rather than its monetary value.
Quality Time: Offering complete, undivided attention through focused conversations or shared activities without distractions like phones.
Physical Touch: Communicating care and security through physical connection, such as holding hands, hugging, or cuddling
Acts of Service: Doing helpful tasks or chores that lighten your partner's physical or mental load, showing that actions speak louder than words.
Jesus showed us what love looks like. He came to serve. And as His followers, we simply follow His example.
In the church, one important way we love one another is by using the gifts God has given us to serve others.
Romans 12:6–8 — “We have different gifts, according to the grace given to each of us… if it is serving, then serve; if it is teaching, then teach; if it is to encourage, then give encouragement… if it is to lead, do it diligently; if it is to show mercy, do it cheerfully.”
God has given each of us different gifts. We are gifted differently, but we are gifted for one another.
So to everyone who serves in our church—to our FG leaders, ushers, greeters, worship team, intercessors, counselors, teachers, elders, pastors, and all our volunteers—thank you!
Every time you use what God has given you to serve His people, you are expressing your love for the church.
And i would like to continue to encourage all of us who serve.
Paul says:
Romans 12:11 — “Never be lacking in zeal, but keep your spiritual fervor, serving the Lord.”
What does that mean?
“Never be lacking in zeal” simply means: Don't become lazy or careless in serving. Stay eager. Stay diligent. Give your best.
And “keep your spiritual fervor” carries the picture of staying spiritually on fire—keeping the flame burning.
Because the reality is, our passion to serve can diminish.
We can get tired.
We can get discouraged.
We can become too busy.
We can become disappointed.
Sometimes serving simply becomes routine.
And slowly, we can lose our zeal and spiritual fervor.
But Paul reminds us of something important: We are “serving the Lord.”
Ultimately, we are serving Jesus.
Yes, we serve people. We serve the church. But behind every person we serve, we are serving the Lord.
So don't serve half-heartedly. Serve with eagerness. Serve with passion. Serve faithfully.
And perhaps this is something we need to ask ourselves today:
What is affecting my zeal and passion to serve the Lord?
And more importantly:
What do I need to do to keep the fire burning?
Maybe you need to rest.
Maybe you need to reconnect with God.
Maybe you need encouragement from others.
Maybe you need to remember why you started serving in the first place.
Whatever it may be, don't just keep serving while your heart grows cold. Come back to Jesus. Let Him renew your heart.
Because we don't simply want busy hands serving Jesus. We want hearts that are still burning for Jesus.
Keep serving. Keep growing. Keep the fire burning.
LOVING ONE ANOTHER…
B: SHARING: It is sharing and blessing one another.
Romans 12:13 — “Share with the Lord’s people who are in need. Practice hospitality.”
Romans 12:14 — “Bless those who persecute you; bless and do not curse.”
One way we show that we belong to one another is by sharing what God has given us.
Paul gives us three simple ways to share and bless others.
1. SHARE WHAT YOU HAVE
“Share with the Lord’s people who are in need.”
When we see a brother or sister in need, we don't simply say, “I’ll pray for you.” We ask, “Is there something I can do to help?”
Maybe someone needs food. Maybe someone is struggling financially. Maybe someone needs transportation or some other practical help.
God blesses us not only so that we can enjoy His blessings, but also so that we can become a blessing to others.
Ask yourself:
Is there someone in my FG, church, or circle of relationships who has a need that God is calling me to help meet?
2. SHARE YOUR HOME
“Practice hospitality.”
Hospitality is simply opening our lives and making room for others.
It doesn't require a beautiful house or an expensive meal. Sometimes it's simply saying, “Come over. Let's eat. Let's talk. You are welcome here.”
Our homes can become places where people are loved, encouraged, discipled, and cared for.
Don't just open your door. Open your life.
3. SHARE WORDS THAT BLESS
“Bless those who persecute you; bless and do not curse.”
We also share through our words.
Words can hurt or heal, discourage or encourage, tear down or build up.
And Paul goes even further: we are not only to bless people who are good to us. We are called to bless even those who hurt us.
Instead of returning hurt for hurt, criticism for criticism, or angry words for angry words, Jesus calls us to respond differently.
Speak words that give grace.Speak words that encourage.Speak words that bless.
This is what One Another Christianity looks like: We share!
Share what you have.Share your home.Share words that bless.
Because when we understand that we belong to one another, what God has given me is no longer only about me.
LOVING ONE ANOTHER
A: SERVING: It is serving  the Lord and one another.
B: SHARING: It is sharing and blessing one another.
C: EMPATHIZING: It  is empathizing with one another.
Romans 12:15 — “Rejoice with those who rejoice; mourn with those who mourn.”
Christian love means entering into what other people are going through.
We don't just live beside one another. We share life with one another.
Paul says: “Rejoice with those who rejoice.”
When something good happens to someone, celebrate with them.
When someone gets a promotion, celebrate.When someone's prayer is answered, celebrate.When someone succeeds, celebrate.When God blesses someone, celebrate!
Sometimes that's not easy.
Why? Because when someone receives something we have been praying for ourselves, we can begin to compare.
“Lord, how about me?”“Why did they get it and I didn't?”
But genuine love says, “Your blessing may not be my blessing, but I can still celebrate with you.”
When we truly love one another, we don't compete with each other—we celebrate each other.
Then Paul says: “Mourn with those who mourn.”
When someone is hurting, hurt with them.
Sometimes we think we need to have the right words. We want to explain what happened, give advice, solve the problem, or immediately make the person feel better.
But sometimes people don't need our answers. They simply need our presence.
Sometimes the most loving thing we can do is to sit beside someone and say:
“I'm here. You're not going through this alone.”
That's empathy.
Your joy matters to me.Your pain matters to me.What you're going through matters to me.
This is what happens when we understand that we belong to one another.
So here's a simple challenge:
Who around you needs you to celebrate with them?
And...
Who around you is hurting and simply needs you to be there?
Celebrate with one another.Cry with one another.Walk with one another.
LOVING ONE ANOTHER
D: LIVING IN HARMONY: It is living in harmony with one another.
Romans 12:16 — “Live in harmony with one another.”
There are many ways we can live in harmony with one another. But Paul points to one important attitude that can destroy harmony: pride.
Pride makes us think, “I am better than you.” And when we think we are better than others, we begin choosing whom we will accept, associate with, or even love.
But Paul says, “Do not think you are superior.”
In the church, we don't look down on people because of their social status, education, job, background, appearance, personality, or what they can offer us.
There should be no “higher” or “lower” people among us. We all stand together under the grace of God.
So instead of avoiding people whom the world considers unimportant, we welcome them. We include them. We associate with them. We treat them with dignity and respect.
That's what harmony looks like.
Don't look down on others.
Don't discriminate.
Don't think you are better.
Welcome and value everyone.
Because when we understand that we belong to one another, there is no room for superiority.
E: DOING WHAT IS RIGHT: It is doing what is right, instead of taking revenge.
We avoid taking vengeance and instead seek to do what is right and good to everyone.
Romans 12:17 — “Do not repay anyone evil for evil. Be careful to do what is right in the eyes of everyone.”
Romans 12:18 — “If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone.”
Romans 12:19 — “Do not take revenge, my dear friends, but leave room for God’s wrath.”
What do we do when someone hurts us, mistreats us, or does something wrong against us?
Our natural response may be to get even. If they hurt us, we want them to hurt too.
But a transformed life responds differently.
Romans 12:17 — “Do not repay anyone evil for evil. Be careful to do what is right in the eyes of everyone.”
Paul says, don't answer evil with more evil. Choose to do what is right.
Romans 12:18 — “If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone.”
We cannot control how other people respond to us. But we can control how we respond to them. As far as it depends on us, we choose peace.
Then Paul says:
Romans 12:19 — “Do not take revenge, my dear friends, but leave room for God’s wrath.”
We don't need to take matters into our own hands. We can leave justice in God's hands.
But Paul goes even further. It is not enough simply to avoid revenge. We actively choose to do good—even to those who have hurt us.
Romans 12:20 — “If your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him something to drink.”
Instead of asking, “How can I get even?” we ask, “What is the right and good thing to do?”
And Paul ends the chapter with this powerful challenge:
Romans 12:21 — “Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.”
When we repay evil with evil, evil wins twice. Their wrongdoing causes us to respond with wrongdoing.
But when we respond to evil by doing what is right and good, we refuse to let someone else's wrong determine the kind of person we become.
This is the transformed life:
Don't get even.
Choose peace.
Leave justice to God.
Do good—even to those who hurt you.
OVERCOME EVIL WITH GOOD.
Who has hurt or offended you—and what would it look like for you to choose what is right and good toward that person?
CONCLUSION
Romans 12 shows us that because of God’s grace and mercy in Christ, the gospel transforms us from being self-centered to other-centered. As our minds are renewed, we grow in humility and recognize that in Christ we are one body and belong to one another.
This is what we call One Another Christianity.
One another Christianity is marked by love.
Nag-iisip ka ba kung paano mo ipapakita ang pag ibig sa kapwa mo?
Loving one another means:
- Serving the Lord and one another.
- Sharing and blessing one another.
- Empathizing with one another—celebrating their joys and sharing their pain.
- Living in harmony with one another.
- Doing what is right and good instead of taking revenge.
In short: Serve. Share. Care. Live in peace. Do good.
Jesus brought us to God—and brought us together—so that we may live not only for ourselves, but for one another.
